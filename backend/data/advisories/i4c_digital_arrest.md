*Plain-language summary of public guidance from the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), Ministry of Home Affairs. Not an official I4C or MHA publication.*

## No law lets anyone arrest you over a call

Nobody in India can arrest you through a phone call or a video call. There is no such procedure. The words "digital arrest" were invented by criminals. If a caller says you are under arrest and must stay on the call, that caller is a thief, not an officer.

Police, CBI, ED, customs and the courts do not work this way. They do not question people over video call. They do not keep someone online for hours. They do not tell you to hide the call from your family. And no real officer will ever ask you to send money to prove that you are innocent.

## The stories these callers use

The story changes, the plan stays the same. You may hear that a parcel in your name was caught with drugs, that your bank account or SIM was used for money laundering, or that a case has been filed against you in a far-off city. Some callers wear a uniform on camera. Some show a room made to look like a police station or a court.

Then comes the real ask. They tell you to move your savings to a "verification account" or an "RBI account" and promise to send it back after the checking is over. That money is gone the moment you send it. RBI does not keep anyone's money in such an account.

## What to do while the call is happening

- Cut the call. You are allowed to hang up. Nothing bad happens because you did.
- Do not switch on your camera, and do not move the call to another app.
- Do not send money to any account, no matter how official the reason sounds.
- Tell one family member or neighbour right away. Secrecy is this scam's main tool.
- Call 1930, the national cybercrime helpline, and file a report on cybercrime.gov.in.

## If you have already paid

Call 1930 the same day, even at night. The sooner the bank and the police know, the better the chance of stopping the money. Report it on cybercrime.gov.in as well, and keep the call records, the account number you paid to, and any screenshots.

Being frightened by such a call is normal. These callers are trained to frighten people. Nobody thinks clearly while a stranger is shouting about jail. If you are unsure, take your phone to your bank branch or the nearest police station and show them everything before you do anything else.
