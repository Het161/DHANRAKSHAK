*Plain-language summary of public guidance from the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), Ministry of Home Affairs. Not an official I4C or MHA publication.*

## The KYC message that wants you to hurry

A message arrives saying your KYC has expired and your account will be blocked today. It gives a link to tap, or tells you to install an app to finish the KYC. Banks do not close accounts by SMS, and they do not send a link to fix your KYC.

Look at the link before you believe it. Real bank addresses are short and end with the bank's own name. Scam links are shortened so you cannot see where they go, or they wrap the bank's name inside a longer address, or they change one letter so the page only looks like the bank's page. If your KYC is really due, your branch will tell you at the counter or on a call you can check for yourself.

## The prize that asks you for money

You are told you have won a lottery, a lucky draw, a phone, or a job in another city. First you must send a small processing fee, a courier charge, a customs duty, or a registration amount. There is no prize and there is no job. The fee is the scam. After the first payment the caller will ask for a second one, then a third.

A genuine prize never needs money from the winner. A genuine employer never asks you to pay to be hired.

## Apps, screen sharing and APK files

A caller says he is a bank executive and wants to help you. He asks you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport or a similar app so he can see your screen. Once it is on, he can watch you type your PIN and can operate your phone himself. Other scammers send a file on WhatsApp whose name ends in .apk and ask you to install it. Such a file can read your SMS and take your OTPs.

## What to do

- Do not open a link that came with a KYC warning. Open your own bank app, or walk into the branch.
- Never install an app because a caller told you to, and never install a file sent on WhatsApp.
- Never pay a fee to receive a prize, a refund, or a job.
- If you already installed a screen sharing app, uninstall it, turn off your phone's internet, and call your bank.
- Report the number and the message on 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in, and keep the message as proof.
