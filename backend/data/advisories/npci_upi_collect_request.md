*Plain-language summary of public guidance from the National Payments Corporation of India. Not an official NPCI publication.*

## The one rule that never changes

Your UPI PIN only sends money out. It never brings money in.

Money coming into your account needs nothing from you. No PIN, no OTP, no approval, no new app. It simply arrives and your bank sends you a credit message. So if anyone says "enter your PIN to receive the refund" or "approve this and your prize money will come", that person is taking money out of your account, not putting it in. This is true on PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm, BHIM and every other UPI app.

## What this trap looks like on your phone

The scammer uses a collect request. It is a normal UPI feature that lets one person ask another for money. Used as a trap, it looks like this:

1. A notification arrives, such as "Payment request received" or "Rahul is requesting Rs. 25,000".
2. You open the app and see a large PAY or ACCEPT button.
3. The sender's name may read "SBI Refund", "Cashback", "OLX Delivery" or "Insurance Claim". Anyone can type that name into their own profile. Your bank does not check it.
4. If you press pay, the app asks for your four or six digit UPI PIN.
5. The moment you enter the PIN, the money leaves your account. It cannot be pulled back.

## Check these things before you press anything

- Read the direction word on the screen. "Paying", "Pay", "Debit" or "Sending" all mean money is leaving your account.
- Treat every PIN screen as a payment screen. There is no exception.
- Look at the amount. It is usually the exact sum you were promised as a refund or prize.
- Do not trust the display name. Tell the person to just send the money, then wait for your bank's credit SMS.
- If you did not ask anyone for money, decline the request and block that UPI ID.

## If you have already entered your PIN

- Open the transaction in your UPI app and use Report or Raise Dispute, then block the sender's UPI ID.
- Call your bank on the number printed on the back of your ATM card or in your passbook and ask them to raise a UPI dispute.
- Call 1930, the national cybercrime helpline, the same day. Money reported in the first few hours is easiest to freeze.
- File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in with the UPI ID, the amount and the transaction reference number.
- Never pay again to "release" or "unblock" the first payment. That is the same scam continuing.
