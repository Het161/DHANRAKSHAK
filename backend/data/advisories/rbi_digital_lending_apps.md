*Plain-language summary of public guidance from the Reserve Bank of India. Not an official RBI publication.*

Money runs short in every household. Needing a loan is normal and it is nothing to be ashamed of. This page is only about telling a real lender apart from a trap.

## Borrow only from a lender the RBI regulates

In India a loan can legally be given only by a bank or by a Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) registered with the Reserve Bank of India. An app is not a lender. An app is only a shopfront for one.

Before you accept any money, the app must tell you in writing which bank or NBFC is actually giving the loan, the full rate of interest, and every fee. If the app will not name its lender, walk away. Ask for the loan agreement before the money reaches you, not after. A genuine lender gives it without argument. You can check whether a company is registered on the RBI website, rbi.org.in.

## Apps that ask for your contacts and your photos

A real loan app does not need your phone contacts, your photo gallery, or your camera to decide your loan. An app that demands these permissions is collecting people it can threaten later.

Never install a screen-sharing or remote-control app such as AnyDesk, TeamViewer or QuickSupport because a loan officer asked you to. That hands a stranger full control of your phone and your bank app.

## Threats and morphed photos are a crime

Some recovery agents abuse borrowers, call their relatives, or paste a borrower's face onto an obscene picture and send it to their contacts. Be clear about this: that is a criminal offence committed against you. It is not part of any loan. Even if you genuinely owe money, no one is permitted to do it. You have not done anything wrong, and the shame belongs entirely to the person sending those messages.

## What to do

- Stop paying anything that is demanded by threat. Pay only into the lender's official account, exactly as written in your agreement.
- Do not delete the calls, messages or images. Take screenshots. They are your evidence.
- Report it on the cybercrime helpline 1930, or online at cybercrime.gov.in.
- File a written complaint at your local police station and keep a stamped copy.
- Complain against the lender on the RBI Sachet portal, sachet.rbi.org.in.
- Tell one family member or neighbour today. Facing it alone is exactly what the agent is counting on.
