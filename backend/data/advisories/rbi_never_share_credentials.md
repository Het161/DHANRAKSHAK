*Plain-language summary of public guidance from the Reserve Bank of India. Not an official RBI publication.*

## Some numbers belong only to you

Your OTP belongs only to you. So does your ATM PIN, your UPI PIN, the three-digit
CVV on the back of your card, your full card number and your internet banking
password.

A real bank employee never needs any of these from you. The bank already has your
account details on its own computer. So if someone on a call or on WhatsApp asks
you for these numbers, that person does not work for your bank, whatever name or
post they give.

This stays true even when the caller sounds convincing:

- The caller already knows your name, your village or part of your account number.
- The message came from a number or a sender name that looks official.
- The caller says your account will be blocked in ten minutes.
- The caller says they are calling from the Reserve Bank of India.

## The Reserve Bank of India does not call customers

RBI is the bank for banks. It does not open accounts for ordinary people. It has
no reason to phone you, message you, or send you a link about your account, KYC
or money. Anyone who says "I am from RBI" and then asks for a number or a
transfer is lying to you.

## What to do when such a call or message comes

- Do not say any secret number out loud, not even one digit.
- Cut the call. You do not owe politeness to a stranger who is pressuring you.
- Turn your ATM card over and call the customer care number printed on the back,
  or the number printed inside your passbook. Use only that number.
- If you are still unsure, walk into your branch and ask at the counter.
- Never open a link sent in a message about your account. Use only the bank app
  you already installed.

## Hurry is itself the warning sign

Real bank work is never a ten-minute emergency. Blocking a card, restarting KYC
and getting a refund can all wait until you have checked. Fear and hurry are the
caller's tools, so slowing down breaks the trick.

If you have already shared an OTP, a PIN or a card number, act the same day. Call
your bank on the number on your card and ask them to block the card or freeze the
account. Then report it on the national cybercrime helpline 1930, or at
cybercrime.gov.in. Reporting fast gives the bank the best chance of stopping the
money. You have done nothing wrong by being tricked.
